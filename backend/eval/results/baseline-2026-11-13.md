- **Gemma beats Qwen in 7 of 8** (`tr` a tie, n=31), so the ordering does not turn on which

**The LLM's advantage is coverage, not accuracy.** `today` produced a voiceable pronunciation for
every one of 2,507 names. `oracle` refuses **16 Vietnamese** names on tone digits; `floor`
refuses **25** (18 Arabic, 7 Vietnamese). At a ceremony each of those is a participant with no
clip.

So the fork 04a informs is now decided in shape: **choosing the right voice is worth more than
asking a model for IPA directly — but only for the names that voice will answer at all.** Brief
04's design (an LLM picks the language, eSpeak phonemizes) is the one these numbers support, and
it inherits `oracle`'s refusals unless the guard work ships with it.

⚠️ **Four cautions, every one measured here, not assumed.**

1. **`oracle` is not a thing production can have.** It is handed the dataset's own `lang` label.
   A real system must infer the language, so `oracle` bounds what a *perfect* identifier would
   buy, not what a real one will. It is a reference point, not a ceiling — §"Headroom" shows a
   hindsight best-of-two beating it in 6 of 8 languages.
2. **The LLM path is not reproducible, and the app's code says it is.** At `temperature: 0`,
   **35.6%** of names returned different IPA across three identical re-runs — see §"The same
   request, three different answers". Every `today` figure here is a single draw.
3. **`de`'s floor-vs-oracle gap is overstated.** panphon cannot read the `ɚ` that en-us eSpeak
   writes for German `-er`, so the floor is charged a full miss for a near-perfect match;
   re-scoring `ɚ` as `ɐ` moves `de`'s headroom from 69% to 62%.
4. **`ar` is in native script and `pl` is 56% of the corpus.** Neither is roster-realistic. The
   renderer's own caveats at the end of §"Reading these numbers" apply to every row above.

**Cost of the whole measurement: $0.1889**, against a $0.25 ceiling agreed before the first
scored call.

## Error per reference segment

**Not a rate, and `ceiling` is not a maximum.** `ceiling` is the cost
of saying *nothing* — a flat ~7.25 per reference segment, measured.
An output *longer* than the reference exceeds it without bound,
because every extra segment is an insertion. So **a figure above
`ceiling` is itself a diagnostic**: it means the engine said far too
much, which in this corpus is always eSpeak narrating a script it
cannot read rather than pronouncing a name.

In this run 114 name-and-engine row(s) exceed their own ceiling — a name counted once per engine that over-produces for it, since `n_over_ceiling` is per cell. The worst is `عيسى` under `ar`/`floor` at 75.125 against a ceiling of 7.25 — 10.4x the cost of saying nothing.

## Characters panphon could not read

After normalisation, so these are genuinely unreadable rather than
notation. A symbol here is invisible to the distance, which is why
it gets its own column instead of being folded into the error.

**Measured asymmetry — the error figure is *blind* to these, not
lenient, and the dominant direction is to over-charge.** `ɚ`, `ᵻ`, `(`
and `)` are all *in* Kokoro's 114-symbol vocabulary but *not*
segmentable by panphon, so they reach audio while being invisible to
the distance. Invisible is not free, and which way it cuts depends on
whether the character sits opposite a reference segment:

- **Standing in for a segment, it costs the maximum.** The reference
  segment it should have matched is left unmatched, so the distance
  charges a full 7.25 deletion where a wrong-but-readable phoneme
  would have cost a fraction of it. German is this case almost every
  time: en-us
  eSpeak writes `-er` as `ɚ` and the German reference writes `ɐ`, a
  near-perfect match scored as a total miss (`ˈɛkɚt` vs `ˈɛkɐt`:
  1.8125 per segment, where a readable-but-wrong vowel such as `ɪ`
  would have cost 0.1875). So wherever this column is non-empty for
  `floor`, the floor is **punished** and the headroom **overstated**.
  How much is not derivable here: this renderer sees only cells, so a
  count of affected names would be a number it cannot measure. Re-score
  the rows if you need one.
- **Appended where the reference has nothing, it is free.** That is the
  minority case, and it is what happens inside narration, where losing
  one segment out of thirty-four helps.
- **A language-switch flag over-charges outright.** The parentheses are
  invisible but the letters inside them are not: `(en)ˈabi(fr)` segments
  as `e n a b i f r`, so the flag adds phantom phonemes at full
  insertion cost. `tts.prepare_ipa` and the vocabulary filter pass the
  whole flag through untouched and `orchestrator.ipa_refusal` does not
  refuse it, so it reaches audio too — but most of what it costs in
  *this* table is markup, not mispronunciation.

So read a cell holding these symbols as **an upper bound on the
engine's error, loosest where the output length is about right**. The
direction is not uniform, which is why these characters are counted
here instead of being folded into the error.

- **`ar`/`today`**: `̄`×4
- **`de`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×60, `ᵻ`×1
- **`de`/`today`**: `َ`×1, `ا`×1
- **`es`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×21, `ᵻ`×6
- **`es`/`today`**: `́`×1
- **`fr`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×22, `ᵻ`×5
- **`fr`/`oracle`**: `(`×30, `)`×30, `-`×2
- **`fr`/`today`**: `‿`×1
- **`it`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×16, `ᵻ`×4
- **`pl`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×43, `ᵻ`×8
- **`pl`/`today`**: `ۛ`×1, `ʲ`×1, `́`×1
- **`tr`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×3
- **`vi`/`floor`**: `ɚ`×19
- **`vi`/`oracle`**: `(`×2, `)`×2, `-`×1
- **`vi`/`today`**: `̚`×2, `̂`×1

## Reading these numbers

- **Authenticity, not correctness.** A reference is one Wiktionary
  contributor's transcription; several pronunciations of a name can be
  right, so the scorer takes the closest attested variant and these
  figures rank engines against a fixed yardstick rather than grading
  pronunciations.
- **Not a rate, and unbounded above.** Saying *nothing* costs a flat
  ~7.25 per reference segment, which is the `ceiling` column; saying too
  *much* costs more, without limit, because every extra segment is an
  insertion. So `ceiling` is a reference point and not a maximum, and
  `over ceiling` counts the names that pass it. Dividing by reference
  length also makes the same single error dearer in a short name, which
  is why engines are compared **within** a language and never across.
- **Frequency buckets are a weak signal.** Every name here is on
  Wiktionary by construction and `wordfreq` returns 0.0 for a token
  absent from running text, so `rare` mostly means `not in running
  text`, not `rare as a name`.
- **`ar` is in native script, not roster-realistic.** No organizer
  types Arabic script into a roster, so `ar` measures what eSpeak
  does with a script rather than what a ceremony would face.
- **`pl` dominates the corpus (56% of this run's names).** Per the dataset card: "Polish IPA on Wiktionary is largely template-generated from spelling (1.01 variants per name, against 2.47 for Spanish). Treat very high Polish scores as evidence about a transliteration rule, not about names."

`qwen/qwen3-4b` (confirmed **not served** by OpenRouter on 2026-11-13).

### ⚠️ Qwen's first run measured the harness, not the model

Run as shipped, `qwen/qwen3.5-9b` returned **2,504 `llm_empty` of 2,507** (99.9%), 2
`llm_error`, 1 `llm_accepted`. It is a reasoning model that reasons by default: the probe shows
`finish_reason: length`, `completion=256`, `reasoning=256`, `content: None` — it spends the
entire `max_tokens` budget thinking and never emits an answer. Every one of those rows fell
through to the eSpeak floor, so that run's `today` column was a measurement of `floor` wearing
the model's name.

The branch ledger is what caught it, and it is the reason Amendment B exists: the error figures
alone looked unremarkable, because falling through to the floor produces *floor-shaped numbers*.

`{"reasoning": {"enabled": false}}` fixes it (and `{"reasoning": {"effort": "none"}}` also
works); `{"chat_template_kwargs": {"enable_thinking": false}}` does **not** on this model. The
figures above are the re-run with reasoning disabled, recorded in that run's provenance as a
modified request. **Process note:** the probe was run against M1 only and then both models were
launched. Probing each model before its scored run — which the brief already requires — would
have caught this for the price of five calls instead of 2,507.

## The same request, three different answers

`gemma.py:268` sends `"temperature": 0` with the comment `# deterministic -- prep re-runs are
stable`. It is not, and this matters to every figure above.

Three identical `generate` runs over the same 104 dev names, same model, same temperature:

| | |
|---|---|
| names run three times | 104 |
| identical all three times | 67 (64.4%) |
| **returned different IPA** | **37 (35.6%)** |

Spread evenly across languages — ar 5/13, de 3/13, es 7/13, fr 5/13, it 6/13, pl 4/13, tr 4/13,
vi 3/13 — so it is not one language's quirk. Nor is it only notation:

| name | answers seen |
|---|---|
| `Adenauer` (de) | `ˈadənaʊər` / `ˈɑːdənɔɪər` |
| `إياد` (ar) | `ijad` / `ijˈaːd` |
| `العوادي` (ar) | `al.ʕə.waːdiː` / `alʕəwəːdiː` |
| `جلال` (ar) | `dʒəˈlaːl` / `dʒəˈlāl` |

`Adenauer`'s two readings are different pronunciations, not different spellings of one. And
`dʒəˈlāl` contains `ā`, a Latin letter with a macron, which is not IPA at all.

**What it does to the numbers.** On these 13-name cells the error figure moved between 0.7%
(`fr`) and 29.2% (`it`) of its own mean across the three runs. A cell mean steadies as it grows,
so the eight test-split cells — n=31 to n=1,407 — are far more stable than that; but the
instability bites hardest exactly where n is smallest, which here is `tr` (31), `vi` (37) and
`ar` (57) — two of them the cells carrying a "no difference" verdict. **The six decided verdicts
have gaps of 0.51 to 0.92 and are not at risk; the two ties are the ones to re-measure** before
anything is built on them. Repeat runs at full scale were not in budget.

⚠️ The likely mechanism is OpenRouter routing identical requests to providers with differing
quantisations, but that is **unverified** — `probe` does not record the served provider. The
eSpeak engines, by contrast, reproduced bit-for-bit across two separate runs, so the
non-determinism is the model's and not the harness's.

**Fenced:** the stale comment, and whether to pin a provider or a seed, belong to the brief that
owns `gemma.py`. 04a changes no application behaviour.

## What the model costs at the Kokoro boundary

`today` has its own out-of-vocabulary problem, different in kind from eSpeak's — it invents
symbols rather than using precise ones. Counted over all 2,507 Gemma rows:

| stage | symbols |
|---|---|
| folded away by `prepare_ipa` | `͡` ×285 (pl), `͡` ×3 + `ً` ×1 + `̩` ×1 (de), `ā` ×4 (ar), `̞`/`̠` ×4 (fr), `̚`/`̯`/`â`/`͡` (vi), `í` (es), `̯` ×8 + `ۛ` + `ć` (pl) |
| dropped by the 114-set | `ʐ` ×89, `ʑ` ×32, `ɫ` ×15, `g` ×3 (pl); `ʕ` ×8, `ħ` ×7, `ˤ` ×4, `g` ×1 (ar); `ʏ` (de, fr, tr), `ا` + `ɦ` (de), `ʀ` + `‿` (fr), `˦`/`˥`/`ɗ` (vi) |

Three things worth naming:

- **It writes ASCII `g` where IPA wants `ɡ`.** U+0067 is *not* in Kokoro's 114-symbol set;
  U+0261 is. So the model spells the sound correctly to a human reader and the consonant is then
  deleted from the clip without a word. Four occurrences here — small, but silent and total.
- **It leaks the wrong script.** An Arabic letter `ا` and an Arabic vowel mark `ً` appear in
  *German* outputs; `ۛ` appears in Polish.
- **The tie bar is harmless.** `͡` ×285 in Polish is `t͡s`-style affricate notation; folding it
  leaves `ts`, which Kokoro can voice.

Against eSpeak's losses this is modest, and `chars dropped/name` stays at or below 0.07 in every
language. But it is a different failure: eSpeak emits a phoneme Kokoro lacks, while the model
emits a character that is not IPA.

### R1 missed, and the first explanation of the miss was also wrong

The prediction's basis was two names, both refused for a tone digit. The corrected reading, after
probing eSpeak directly rather than only reading this corpus:

| Tone | eSpeak writes | Names here | Refused? |
|---|---|---|---|
| *sắc* (acute) | the vowel **`ɜ`** | 6 | ❌ **no — and never, for any syllable probed** |
| *huyền* (grave) | digit `2` | 6 | ✅ `contains the digit '2'` |
| *hỏi* (hook) | digit `4` | 1 | ✅ |
| *ngã* (tilde) | digit `5` | 1 | ✅ |
| *nặng* (dot below) | digit `6` | 8 | ✅ |
| *ngang* (level, unmarked) | **usually nothing — but digit `1` in the `âu`/`êu` rimes** | 15 | ❌ not here; ✅ for a `âu`/`êu` name |

Each name is counted once, under the notation that decided its outcome: `Thiết Mộc Chân` carries
both *sắc* and *nặng* and is booked to *nặng*, so **7** outputs contain `ɜ` while 6 of them go
unrefused.

⚠️ **An earlier draft of this file claimed the mapping was exceptionless over 37/37 names and
that "eSpeak's Vietnamese is refused for exactly four of its six tones". Both were wrong**, and
the corpus is what hid it. *ngang* is not silent in general — eSpeak writes an explicit tone
digit `1` for the `âu` and `êu` rimes, and **no name in either split has that rime**:

```
espeak_ipa("chau",  "vi") -> tʃˈaw     # no diacritic at all: nothing in the tone slot
espeak_ipa("châu",  "vi") -> tʃˈə1w    # same ngang tone, â rime: digit 1
espeak_ipa("chấu",  "vi") -> tʃˈəɜw    # sắc
espeak_ipa("chầu",  "vi") -> tʃˈə2w    # huyền ... and so on through 4, 5, 6
```

`â` is a vowel-quality mark, not a tone mark, so the digit tracks the **rime**, not the tone.
`Châu` is a common Vietnamese given name and surname, and `orchestrator.ipa_refusal` refuses it:
`contains the digit '1', which Kokoro reads aloud`. The corrected rule: **`sắc` is the only tone
that never produces a refusal; the other five all can — four always, *ngang* in the `âu`/`êu`
rimes.** So 43.2% is this corpus's tone *and rime* mix, and a realistic roster's rate would be
**higher**, not a fixed function of tone.

**What survives, and is the actual finding:** *sắc* is written `ɜ` and never a digit — 140 of
144 probed syllables (12 nuclei × 12 codas), and the 4 misses are illegal rimes (`íi`, `úu`,
`ýi`, `ýu`) that eSpeak reduces to a glide with no vowel to carry a tone at all. The guard's
digit test
cannot see it, and **`ɜ` is in Kokoro's 114-symbol vocabulary** — so unlike the dental diacritics
it is not filtered out; it reaches the model as a token of its own and the name gets a clip.
*What that token sounds like is untested* — nothing in this work renders audio — so the claim is
"the guard passes it and a clip is produced", not "it is voiced as an extra vowel".

**One more name in that table is wrong to call safe.** `Phri-đrich` sits in the *ngang* row with
nothing to refuse, but its output is `fzˈi(en)dɹˈɪtʃ(vi)` — eSpeak language-switch markers
written into the IPA. `tts.prepare_ipa` leaves them intact, every character of them is in the
114-symbol vocabulary, and `ipa_refusal` returns `None`, so Kokoro is handed `(en)` and `(vi)` as
phonemes. The scorer already singles it out: `n_language_switch_flags: 1` and vi/oracle's
`raw_max_name`. The guard has no rule for it. Owner: the guard fix, not 04a.

### R3's 18 refusals are one Arabic letter, and the guard's narration test does nothing here

Two corrections to how the Arabic result reads, neither of which changes R3's verdict.

**The 18 refusals are the names containing د.** Across the whole `ar`/`floor` cell the only digit
emitted is `1`, 21 occurrences, all inside the token `ˈæɹəbɪkdˈæl1` ("Arabic dal"). The set of
names containing that token *is* the refused set, exactly. Probing the whole alphabet, only د and
ذ narrate with a digit at all, and ذ does not occur in this split. So read 18/57 as a corpus
accident — 18 of these names happen to contain د — and not as a rate that would hold for another
Arabic roster.

**The guard escapes Arabic for a lexical reason, not a positional one.** `tts.py`'s only
narration rule is a two-item marker set, `_NARRATION_MARKERS = {"lˈɛɾɚ", "lˌɛɾɚ"}` — eSpeak's
English word *letter* — matched against the tail of any token. eSpeak uses that word for Han
characters (李雷 → `tʃˈaɪniːzlˌɛɾɚ`, "Chinese letter") but **never for Arabic**: it names each
letter individually, `ˈæɹəbɪksˈiːn` = "Arabic seen". Measured over the 57 rows: occurrences of
either marker **0**, occurrences of the bare substring `ɛɾɚ` **0**. So token position is not what
lets Arabic through — relaxing token-finality to match anywhere would still catch **0 of 57**.
The signal the guard has no rule for is the *leading* script name `ˈæɹəbɪk`, which prefixes all
302 tokens in the cell. A fix aimed at token position would catch nothing and look like it had
worked.

### R4 holds, and names a cost Brief 04 would be buying

Deltas are measured **within one name set** — the shared voiced set, which for `ar` is 39 of its
57 names and for `vi` is 19 of its 37. (An earlier cross-set reading compared `ar`'s 57 raw names
against those 39 and is not a like-for-like delta.) ⚠️ The two symbol columns are counted over
**every row of the cell, refused rows included**, so they do not divide into the delta.

There are **two** stages, not one. `tts.prepare_ipa`'s `_normalise_for_kokoro` folds away every
combining mark but U+0303, and only what survives reaches `kokoro/model.py`'s 114-symbol filter:

| cell | raw | Kokoro-side | delta | folded by `prepare_ipa` | dropped by the 114-set |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ar`/oracle | 0.691 | 0.949 | **+0.258** | dental `◌̪`×6 | `ʕ`×12, `ħ`×7 |
| `tr`/oracle | 0.112 | 0.208 | **+0.096** | — | `ɫ`×2 |
| `pl`/oracle | 0.194 | 0.253 | **+0.059** | — | `ʑ`×90 |
| `vi`/oracle | 1.073 | 1.107 | **+0.034** | dental `◌̪`×10 | `ɗ`×2, tone digits ×16, `-`×1 |
| `de`/oracle | 0.576 | 0.587 | **+0.011** | — | `ʏ`×1 |
| `fr`/oracle | 0.619 | 0.619 | **+0.000** | — | `-`×2 |
| `es`,`it`/oracle | — | — | **+0.000** | — | nothing |
| **`de`/floor** | 1.830 | 1.832 | **+0.002** | one syllabic `◌̩` (`Karsten`) | — |
| every other `floor` cell | — | — | **+0.000** | — | nothing voiced |

The direction is exactly R4's. Three things the prediction did not say:

**Which engine pays — almost, but not entirely, `oracle`.** Across all 2,507 names `en-us` eSpeak
puts exactly one symbol Kokoro cannot take into an output that gets voiced: the syllabic `◌̩` in
`Karsten`. It is not free — it costs `de`/`floor` **+0.002** (+0.333 on that one name, over 170)
— so "an `oracle`-only cost" is wrong, though by a margin that flips no conclusion. (`en-us` also
writes the digit `1` into Arabic narration, equally out-of-set, but the guard refuses those 18
names, so `ar`/`floor`'s delta is a clean 0.000.) Every *other* out-of-set symbol here comes from
the **correct-language** voice.

**Which stage removes it, and whether that stage is silent.** The dental diacritics and the German
syllabic mark never reach the vocabulary filter at all — `prepare_ipa` folds them first. That
matters because the two stages behave differently when something is lost: the fold **logs**
(`tts.py:233`, `"IPA folded to Kokoro's vocabulary"`, asserted by
`test_synthesize_logs_when_ipa_is_folded`), while the vocabulary filter is the silent one. An
earlier draft of this section called all of it "silently … `kokoro/model.py`", which was wrong
about both.

**That a removed symbol need not cost anything.** `fr`/oracle loses two hyphens and its delta is
exactly +0.000, because panphon cannot read a hyphen either — the symbol is absent from both
sides of the comparison. So the table's right-hand columns are a census of what changes, not of
what is charged for.

The loss is small against the gain (Polish: −2.231 raw, +0.059 back) but it is a cost that today
exists only as +0.002 in one German cell, and `oracle` multiplies it by two orders of magnitude.
It falls hardest on Arabic, the language the floor already fails worst.
